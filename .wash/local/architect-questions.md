## B47 (architect-10 ruling, 2026-10-09, thread B47-stale-close)
Stale `Owned` closes after the kernel freed and reused the index. The kernel's handle word is a bare index (objects.md:70-87, abi.md:47): no generation, and a process closing an index in its own table is within its authority from the kernel's view, so the bug is the VM's bookkeeping across Erlang processes sharing one table. Ruling: option (1), in B47. Rule for beamlet.md "Handles are resource terms": a handle dies with the object it names or the budget that stamped it (objects.md); the VM marks an `Owned` gone when it destroys the budget it came through: the budget's own handle, the budgets the VM carved below it, and every handle returned by calls on a server launched with `serve` under it; a collection then closes nothing at a gone term's index. Residual, stated: a handle revoked outside the VM (a grant's stamp destroyed by the steward while the VM lives) is not known gone; its index may be reused before its term is collected. No `close/1` native: option (1) suffices and a native is surface without need. Tests: host on the fake kernel (which must free the slot and revoke stamps at destruction) and the machine repro (carve, destroy, carve, collect, usage). The kernel generation (a per-slot counter in the handle word, `gen << 12 | index` with MAX_HANDLES 4096, mismatched generation = BadHandle) is the construction fix for the class and the only guard for the residual; it changes the ABI's handle word on both widths and every consumer, so it is the owner's: decision_request sent, recommended as a separate node after B47, not in it.

## SMP6 (architect-10 ruling, 2026-10-09, thread SMP6-zeroing-billed-to-nobody)
Zeroing whose payer is destroyed before the bill: none of (a) nobody, (b) a parent lookup at bill time, (c) the destroyer. A dying budget's pending zeroing debt is lifted with the rest of its debt: the destruction, under the lock, between `mark_dying` and the free, rewrites every hart's zeroing-payer entry naming a dying budget to the destruction's R10 payer (the caller for `budget_destroy`/`budget_reap`; for a deadline the parent after the carve returns, or the nearest ancestor with free weight above 0). Cost: `MAX_HARTS x ZEROING_PAYERS` (4) entries, one dying-flag read each, a constant inside R10's bound; no lookup outside the lock, since the entries are written and read under it. `is_live_budget` at the bill becomes a debug assertion. Only `root`'s destruction leaves a payer with no successor: billed to nobody, with nothing left to run (stated on the page as the one case). B24's nobody share gains nothing from zeroing. Side finding: budgets.md:332-335 ("the kernel departs from this ... bills the rest ... to nobody") contradicts R10 at :639-644 and names a residual that no longer exists; SMP6 removes it as it edits R10.

SMP6 zeroing quota (architect-10 ruling, 2026-10-09, same thread): the quota form. A hart zeroes at most 64 pending frames per kernel exit (about 2 ms under icount, inside the one-section latency bound every interrupt already waits behind), and the same 64 per pass of the idle loop, returning to the loop's interrupt-enabled top between passes; the rest stay in flight and drain on later exits, on idle, and under the lock one at a time when an allocation finds the bitmap empty (the existing rule). R81 and the in-flight invariant are unchanged. The interrupts-enabled alternative is rejected: kernel-mode interrupt re-entry over an abandoned pick is a new trap origin and a bigger mechanism (Tenet 1); it is the noted alternative should the drain prove too slow. Conditions: the bill per exit stays proportional to the frames zeroed that exit, the payer table carrying across exits; the lease-destruction gate records the longest interrupts-off stretch after the change and the exits to drain; memory.md states the quota, the drain, and that a destroyer's caller sees R10's walk, not the zeroing; kernel-red's P2-3 residual takes the new number; the quota is one constant with its reason beside it.

Superseded the same day by measurement (containment deadline_notice p99 31 -> 228 ms: the next runner on the freeing hart paid 67 exits x 2 ms of another's zeroing). Ruling (A): a kernel exit zeroes nothing; an idle hart drains 64 per pass of its loop; an allocation that finds the bitmap empty zeroes one of its own hart's pending frames under the lock, billed as its own kernel time (main's cost, no worse); if it has none and frames are in flight elsewhere, it sets `want` and the owner hart's next entry (the IPI's) zeroes up to 64 before returning, so a busy owner cannot hold another hart's allocation past one quota. Conditions: pending high-water mark and in-flight time under load recorded and on the page; payer table and lift unchanged for idle and the wanted exit; P2-3 residual reads "zeroing runs on idle harts, on demand, and at an exit another hart asked for". The nobody-share rise (23->29, 34->44) is traced before merge.

Want-IPI clause superseded (implementer's form, orchestrator agreed, no objection from me): an allocation finding the bitmap empty takes one pending frame from any hart's list under the lock (`reclaim::pop_pending`), its own first; lists are only ever taken whole; nothing zeroes at an exit. Condition added: every store-back of a list's remainder (the idle owner's, outside the lock; the lock holder's) is a CAS merge onto the current head (the remainder's tail linked to the head seen, then CAS), or taken under the lock, since two store-backs or a store-back against a push would otherwise overwrite a list and leak its frames in flight; ABA is harmless because the tail links to the head node, not through it. Met at c37bc477d by removing store-backs: pending lists are read and written only under the lock; an idle hart cuts up to 64 frames off the front into a private batch while still holding the lock, releases, zeroes and pushes each to done; `pop_pending` pops under the lock. The checked build counts in-flight frames against the ownership table at each destruction's audit, allowing only the in-motion window. Accepted. The payer-table decrement for a frame another hart zeroed is by count, payer unknown per frame; the page says the split is by counts.
