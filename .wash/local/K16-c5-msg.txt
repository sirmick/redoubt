kernel: 511 processes of 255 threads, and every walk follows what exists

The limits grow to the values the system is meant to run with:

  MAX_PROCESS_COUNT   64 -> 511
  MAX_THREADS         31 -> 255
  MAX_OPEN_CALLS      64 -> 256
  MAX_START_HANDLES   64 -> 128
  WAIT_CAP            16 -> 32
  MAX_DEPTH            8 -> 16
  MAX_LABELS           8 -> 16

in redoubt-sys, the kernel and the model's spec alike.

The process limit is held below the ASID field, 2^9 in Sv32 and 2^16 in
Sv39, and PID 0 is never a process, so every PID fits it: once the
kernel flushes by ASID, a process's PID is its ASID with no table or
offset between them. ASID_BITS sits beside MAX_PROCESS_COUNT with a
compile-time assert on each width; 512, ten bits, would not fit Sv32.

A set of TIDs no longer fits a word. bits.rs holds one fixed set,
Bits<W>, walked by set bit. TidMask is Bits<4> and is a process's
allocated threads, its ready threads (ProcessState::Ready and Running)
and its account's live threads. find_next_thread is one function on
both widths, the next ready TID above the current one or else the
lowest, where it had a de Bruijn lookup on rv32.

Every walk of the processes now follows the processes that exist, not
the limit. Before this, a walk read every slot and skipped the empty
ones. At 512 PIDs that failed the containment gate: the bystander's
share fell to 752 of a floor of 783, and budget_destroy took 63 ms.
- The accounts keep live_pids, a PidMask (Bits<8>) of the PIDs with
  an account, set at process_created and cleared at process_ended.
  find_thread, next_timeout, both of budgets_dying's walks,
  sweep_handles, destroy_subtree's victims and holds_process iterate
  it. So do the scheduler's Runnable::fill and next_thread, which
  read the process table by PID; PID 1 has no account, so they no
  longer test for it.
- The PID index of process objects keeps process_pids beside it, set
  and cleared in index_process. find_process (process::budgets_dying
  and endpoints_dying) and migrate_held_pids iterate it. Before,
  migrate_held_pids also copied the whole 4 KiB index onto the stack.
- A checked build audits both sets against a scan of their tables:
  live_pids at every create and end, process_pids with the PID index.
- pids() is left for drawing a free PID and for the checked build's
  handle-chain audit.

The stride queue's scans already visit only queued budgets (the commit
before this one).

On the gate (rv64, qemu seed 13), per destruction, live slots visited
against slots visited, measured at 512 PIDs: find_process 42 of 2,816
before, 42 of 42 now; migrate_held_pids 512 before, 8 now. At 511 the
gate passes: share 829, R10 p50/p99 20,740/25,253 us (18,648/22,383 at
64 PIDs before the values), budget_destroy call to return 55.3 ms.
Most of the rest of that call is the checked build's audit after the
walk (37.9 ms p50, measured before the rebase onto K21), which R10
does not count. decision_wake p50 is 7.7 ms and deadline_notice net p99 28.7 ms.

The kernel's data region grows from 512 KiB to 1 MiB on both widths.
At these values the release kernel's .data and .bss were 464,368 B on
rv64 and left under 64 KiB of the old region. MEMORY_MANAGER alone is
410,312 B on rv64 and 406,144 B on rv32, about 800 B a PID: the
accounts with their handle tables, and the object indexes. link.x and
link64.x give RAM 1024K at the same origin, and assert that the region
ends below the kernel stack's lowest page. The region costs no RAM
until it is used: the loader backs each segment's p_memsz
(loader/src/image.rs), the kernel's allowed range is KERNEL_AREA to the
top of the address space (loader/src/main.rs), and nothing in
libs/layout bounds the region. Now .data + .bss are 468,808 B on rv64
(579,768 B of headroom) and 460,480 B on rv32 (588,096 B).

process_ending's endpoints to pump, one per thread, are a static
(SERVED, .bss). A stack array was 4 KiB of the 32 KiB kernel stack at
255 threads.

The cases follow the values:
- thread-limit counts 255 threads in a set of words.
- process-lifecycle makes 520 exits over PIDs to 511.
- redoubt-ipc abandons 256 calls and counts 256 notices, in up to four
  attempts per open call, as at 64. redoubt-filler offers half the
  bound, which leaves the server room to take the client's abandoned
  calls first.
- budget-test's depth ladder is MAX_DEPTH - 4 levels.
- budget-syscall-attack's over-long label count is MAX_LABELS + 1.
- pid-reuse-authority draws from 510 PIDs: 8,192 tries keep its odds of
  missing A's PID at about e^-16, where 1,024 left them at e^-2, and
  its timeout is 120 s.
- redoubt-sys's own tests: the receive record is 32 slots, and the
  hostile per-kind test fills every label slot and tries a label count
  of MAX_LABELS.
- fsd's argument test gives MAX_LABELS + 1 labels, where it gave a
  fixed nine, which 16 admits.
- init's watcher test lifts root's page limit as it lifts system's
  process limit: at MAX_THREADS - 1 servers their watchers' pages pass
  init's 1,024, and the test is about the threads.
- bundle-mapped checks the boot split from the boot table: root has
  MAX_PROCESS_COUNT - 1 processes, 510, system a quarter, 127, and
  users the rest less init's one, 382, where it pinned 15 and 47.

Two host tests pinned 48, which is MAX_OPEN_CALLS less the headroom at
64. libs/rt's admit.rs tests and servers/ipd's sizing tests now derive
the bound from the two constants. At 256, ipd's budget binds before the
bound does, and the sizing test says so.

The model: a thread bomb lasts MAX_THREADS + 9 steps, and coverage's
and the traces test's random sequences are 450 steps, long enough for
it. Before, a 150-step trace with a bomb never got past it, and
R2NoWaitCap and R4OverdrawOnDelivery replayed unnoticed. The flood's
server budget has a page for each open call, and its crowd is large
enough that more than MAX_OPEN_CALLS calls can queue. With 200 pages
and 3 to 8 accounts, the three R4a mutations went uncaught. The suite
takes 428 s (526 s before).

The pages give the new values: the kernel README, abi (the receive and
budget-spec records and the exit notice at 16 labels), budgets (the
boot table), invariants, ipc (and its walk line), memory-layout (kernel
data 1 MiB), model (the constants and the flood), objects, processes,
scheduling, timer, servers/init and serving, todo/kernel-attack-gaps,
and userland/beamlet, native and sessions. libs/rt's label.rs comment
names MAX_LABELS as 16.

bits.rs has no unsafe and is listed in the kernel's core budget; the
count stays 18.

Size budget: kernel: 8,029 -> 8,081 for the bit sets (bits.rs), the
live-PID and process-object sets with their checked audits, and SERVED,
less the shift masks and rv32's de Bruijn lookup they replace.
Size budget: model: 10,239 -> 10,240 for FLOOD_THREADS, the flood's
fixed 31 threads a process, now apart from MAX_THREADS.
