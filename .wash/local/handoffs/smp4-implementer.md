## What SMP4 put on main
1. hold-trace (kernel feature, implies lock-trace): at each kernel-lock release a traced kernel writes `h` (id = section's first tick, pass = last tick, sched ticks = raw time minus boot) and then `j` (id = cause, pass = audit ticks inside the section). Cause: a syscall's raw number (0x101..0x11c; 0x1ff for an unknown call), 0x200+interrupt code, 0x300+exception code, 0 = kmain. The hooks are cell.rs (acquire_ticket's end calls hold_begin; release calls hold_end), irq.rs (hold_cause after scause), sched.rs trace::{hold_begin,hold_cause,hold_end}. The boot's first section is skipped (it begins before the clock's zero). The oracle prints "kernel sections: N held, ticks net of audits p50/p99/max; longest by cause: ...; lock waits W ticks: behind other harts' sections B (their audits A), the lock free F". Only sched-lock-contention and -4 carry it. Post-check arg fault_section_max_ticks=N bounds the longest page-fault section (causes 0x30c/d/f).
2. kframe::zero: one check, then a volatile store loop (fill()). ~5x faster than the old check-a-word loop.
3. Every whole-table checked-build scan is an audit (AUDIT_INDEXES = 5): check_frame_owners + check_live_pids at process_ended, check_live_pids at an account's making, check_irq_index at index_irq. Inside a destruction they skip (`objects.destroying`); check_object_indexes now covers live PIDs too. sched::audit is a plain check before the timer runs (timebase()==0): boot_budgets runs before arch::init. Without that guard the boot audits stamped spans of thousands of seconds and moved slice ends by garbage.
4. P2, checked builds only: a hart's wait behind another hart's audit is billed to no one. sched::WAITING[hart] = raw came+1, set before the draw (irq.rs). At each audit's end, excuse_waiters adds min(audit length, now - came) to each marked hart's Billing.excused. waited(held, came) clears the mark, zeroes the excuse if !held, and clamps it to the waiter's own clock (harts' time CSRs differ by a few ticks under icount; containment caught it). from_user subtracts the excuse from accrued user time, moves the slice end, and records `y` (id = ticks) right after the Q (or its k). The oracle subtracts only Q-y from shares. Negative feature audit-wait-billed. It CHANGES THE SCHEDULE: an excused waiter keeps its slice and meets the next audit (deadline-flood-billed-traced rv64 moved from 875 to 413 on IRQ1). Written on scheduling.md (share paragraph and residuals).
5. P3: a user fault prints its one PROGRAM HALT line. Registers and print_map print only for a kernel failure or under debug-print. It used to print the whole map, a line per page, under the lock: a 4096-page process held the lock 837/865 ms, now 7.5 ms (its teardown). Case fault-report-bound (log-server, fault-report-witness, fault-report-big; hold-trace; fault_section_max_ticks=150000; forbids '^Memory Maps for', '^Current thread').
6. Docs: the keep table restated (cap-set lifts; a timeout at the first entry); the blind-spot sentence kept with the gross numbers; memory.md says the shootdown wait stays under the lock (0.9 ms over the whole containment gate at 2 harts).

## Measurements (2 harts, icount, checked builds; IRQ1 on main)
- Long sections before SMP4: the destruction audit 20-25 ms (112 ms at containment's full-fill lease); check_frame_owners 7 ms at each process end (it was unstamped); the fault map print; kframe::zero (~220 µs/page; containment: 15 s of zeroing in 68k endpoint_creates); the map_anon whole-area refusal 9.4 ms; a full-area unmap 72 ms and map_device 55 ms (one-off setup in sched-lock-contention); release_owned_frames walk ~1 ms small / 12.5 ms full area; destroy_subtree net of audit 0.8-1 ms small / 23 ms full fill; a timer entry 0.3-0.4 ms per hart per slice.
- After SMP4, the longest net section per case (ticks, 10/µs): budget-churn 89053 -> 28340; exit-churn 158159 -> 25785; share 85005 -> 17045; timer-flood 87324 -> 19231; budget-churn-shell 86860 -> 18769; deadline-flood 83738 -> 15637.
- Shootdown-ack waits: about 0, 0.9 ms over the whole gate. Large copies in the kernel: none (messages are words; pages move by remapping).
- Lock-wait split: in the share cases 77-89 % of the "lock waits" are the lock standing free, the icount hand-off to the halted waiter (~0.5 ms each). Contention case: 98 % behind sections, 1 % free. deadline-flood and budget-churn-shell: ~89 % behind audits (excused).
- Gross judging (billed waits counted as the waiter's) was rejected: exit-churn threads-exit 403/445 gross. Timer-flood is ~499 gross against 522-586 net.

## For SMP6 (zero on reclaim; variant A in SMP4-design.md §5)
- The kernel red's warning: zero a frame only AFTER the shootdown for its last mapping has completed (every asked hart acknowledged). Otherwise a stale-TLB write from another hart can land after the zero and leak into the next owner. Order: clear the entries -> ask and wait for acks (under the lock today) -> retire to IN_FLIGHT -> release -> zero -> lock -> bitmap. Destruction's Leave shootdown already precedes the frees (hart.rs shootdown, SHOOTER is a single static that assumes the asker holds the lock: keep the wait under the lock as ruled).
- Boot frames are not known zero: a dirty twin of bitmap level 0 (zero on first allocation, under the lock as today), or zero all free RAM at boot (measure).
- map_fixed's `.expect("map_fixed: charged for above")` assumes a budget able to pay finds a free frame. With frames in flight, an allocation that finds the bitmap empty must wait for a commit (or zero an unclaimed dirty frame itself). Bound the per-hart reclaim list (e.g. 64) and commit early past it.
- kframe::zero is the only kernel zero outside paging's Window::zero_frame (first touch, header page, page tables use the latter).
- The rule number: R80 was claimed by CTX2. Check SECURITY.md for the next free number at SMP6's merge. The model has one hart: model the commit as a separate event the generator interleaves (free_frame -> in_flight; Event::Commit zeroes -> free_frames). Mutations: in-flight frame left allocatable, commit without zeroing.
- The M2 page's step 5 wording and R11's "zeroed" bullet are SMP6's to reword, by the measurements (cite numbers, not packages).

## For SCHED3 (the 2-hart shares still kept, all deterministic under icount per build; kernel changes flip modes)
- sched-budget-churn-shell: rv64 387, rv32 509 (judged >=450). In the trace: outside audits the shell holds both harts ~60 %. The victim leaves the cap set 80x on rv64 (45x on rv32), each `u` a lift to the floor with no charge, while the shell is reweighed 240x and lifted 80x (child carve/destroy). The lifts total ~572 ms-equivalent on both widths, so the count alone does not settle it. Gross charges: victim 2.97M vs shell 5.41M ticks. Mechanism not proven.
- deadline-flood-billed-traced: bimodal (≈410 or ≈880). rv64 413/402, rv32 516/515 after SMP4; on IRQ1 before SMP4, rv64 875/885.
- sched-wake-no-preempt: no lock waits remain in its intervals. At 2 harts the sleeper's own receive section (~0.5 ms checked) outlasts the 300 µs nap, and the timeout is answered at whichever entry comes first: of 20 naps, 11 at other entries (another hart's syscall or entry), 7 at slice ends (R in the same I..O), 2 mid-slice. A 700 µs nap gives 2/20. The oracle's check_wake_no_preempt needs every nap proven on mid-slice timer intervals, so its proof needs restating for several harts.
- kernel-containment bystander (@1): 463 rv64 / 442 rv32 at 2 harts.
- sched-exit-churn threads-exit (@1): 502/465 at 2 harts in its own config, 441 rv64 with hold-trace's records. Too near 450.

## Traps
- Seed sweeps: these cases do not pin qemu_seed, and --sweep refuses them. They are deterministic per build under icount, so variance comes only from code changes (any kernel change can flip a bimodal share).
- The trace kinds now taken: WRDPKLlefrqwAaGgvNnXYZUVTtIBEOMmCSJHQFukxchjy (x/c are IRQ1's, h/j/y SMP4's). Free: b d i o p s z.
- `j` must follow its `h` on the same hart (check() uses i-2, since its loop index is one past). `y` must follow a Q, or a k after a Q, of its hart. from_user runs before IRQ1's external_entry record, which keeps that order.
- Don't edit a worktree while a cargo testbench there is compiling. A run's console logs are in target/testbench/run-*/, pruned later: copy them at once. The containment ring (256 MiB) overflows with extra per-section records: hold-trace's 2 records per section fit; my scratch part records did not.
- The size budget chains per commit. After any rebase, recount each commit's kernel lines (size-budget at each sha in a scratch worktree) and fix the Size budget lines. GIT_SEQUENCE_EDITOR scripts with exec lines worked for that.
- origin/main moved three times under the fold (CONW1, IRQ1, PIPE1, launch). `git rebase -i origin/main` silently lands on the newer main.
