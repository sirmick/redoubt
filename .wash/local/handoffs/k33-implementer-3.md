## SCHED4: the design's reasoning not in the report
- Why the gate is about deadlines, not timeouts: a timeout's payer is the blocked thread's budget; a one-thread budget runs nowhere while it waits, so its timeout is every hart's by the rule itself (payer nowhere). The count of foreign ticks from timeouts (exit-churn 0.15-0.23 %) is the residual and sits under the 10 per mille bound; deadlines' payer (the creator) runs, so they are placed.
- Why `due()` keeps the global hints (binding 2): I13/R10 order a passed item first at ANY entry; the placement is of the interrupt only. Consequence for the gate: an item due when another hart's slice end goes through kmain (repick's `due()` true) is served on that hart and charged to the payer there, counted by `$` (report-only) not by the gate; hence the bound 10, not 0.
- Why no rearm() in note_*: the WIP patch (33eaaf326) added rearm() in note_timeout/note_budget_deadline; today note_* only record and the entry's return re-arms (one SBI call per exit, B58's gain). With the placement, the noting hart runs the payer (a timeout's blocked thread is the current runner until it blocks, then it runs nowhere -> every hart arms at its own exit; a deadline's parent is the creator, running here) so the return's rearm places it; nothing is lost. kernel-red-2 accepted this.
- The `armed[g] == 0` convention: expire_due and on_interrupt set armed[hart]=0 ("fired, re-arming at the return"); due_on treats `a <= at` as covered, so 0 counts as covered. Sound because g re-arms at its return with the runners as they are then; if g no longer runs the payer, g arms the item itself (payer nowhere, or g sees the payer's other hart armed later -> arms). kernel-red-2 confirmed the argument; P3 (a) asks for a host test of exactly this transient (see P3s).
- Negative construction (`deadline-placement`): the plain deadline-flood program creates 16 budgets with deadlines now+200..215 µs; the creations themselves take ~that long, so most deadlines are served at the creator's own `budget_create` entries (expire_at_entry), not by timer interrupts -> the "91 of 96 on the creator" placement; only phase decides the rest, so timer-any-hart would fail it only by luck (~1 % in the common run). deadline-placement = the same role with deadlines 600 µs ahead (DeadlineFlood p1 = offset; 200 when 0), then the creator spins in user mode with no calls: every deadline is served by a timer interrupt; any-hart: both harts' timers fire at each deadline and icount's turn picks the hart (expect a large share on the victim, each destruction ~9.6k ticks ~0.19 % of a 5M-tick victim pool: 6+ landings fail 10 per mille); B1: only the creator's hart is armed (the round's children share the creator as payer: no tie, as the toml says per binding 1), creator in user mode -> all on its hart. Expected: positive passes, timer-any-hart fails on both widths (quote both in timer.md's TBD-NEG-RV64/RV32 and the toml).
- Oracle arg semantics (binding 2/3): `foreign_timer_in=<share>:<per_mille>`: inside the share's window, timer interrupts FROM USER MODE (I..O) that interrupted the judged budget (root_of marks) and charged a budget outside the marks; ticks over the judged budget's charged ticks (check_hart_share's `its / STRIDE`); judged at harts >= 2, recorded at 1. The `$` report: expiry charges outside interrupts where the hart's runner is under the marks (its own call entries) or runner None with last runner under the marks (kmain after its slice end/block). This is what d24d5b5cf implements; untested.

## K33 kernel facts a SCHED4 implementer must know
- sched::repick (kernel/src/sched.rs ~1030-1105) runs at a slice end in the timer interrupt when time::due() is false and the budget has no other thread waiting; returns Picked::{Own, Other(pid,tid), Kmain}; Own re-picks in place (runner unchanged), Other switches via sched::switch_here (activate_process_thread + sync_icache) in irq.rs after a hart::shot_down() re-check; both then resume through syscall::resume -> sched::leave(pid) which does the switch-on (H record), owed billing, reconcile, the new slice and time::rearm(). Kmain = preempt() round trip; leave(KERNEL_PID) does NOT rearm; kmain's pick -> switch -> leave(n) rearms, or idle rearms.
- The trace: a switching slice end writes `I = R b K n O 0 J H n` (K inside the interrupt, O 0 = "the slice ended at this entry" written by trace::slice_ended()); the round trip `R b H 0 O 0` then `K n` in kmain. Oracle readers were amended for it (carve-return turn, wake-no-preempt proof). `$` (new, WIP) is written by trace::charge when RING.expiry && !interrupt.
- time::due() reads the global hints (.at) and is unchanged by SCHED4 by ruling.
- The timer-entry's kernel sections (hold-trace) are the K33 docs' 930/1,415 ticks; SCHED4's rearm adds runner_ids() (MAX_HARTS reads) per re-arm: expect a few ticks' rise; binding 5 wants the delta reported, bound unchanged.
