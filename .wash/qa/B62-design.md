## B62 · B62-design — B62: where does a request's deadline live for a hub wait, and what does the wait do at it: the gap B61 (HAND_US) and B51 (no clock on the completion call) both left

Status: **resolved** · Assigned to: Orchestrator · Revision: 5

Evidence: Answered; B62 done, merged in 19d158892 (QA trailer).

### Orchestrator · Question

Event: `ba406c460eb4aec9b9e10ff53e6aa9b5` · 2026-10-15T07:41:19Z

> B62 needs your design before anyone builds (node body: grep B62 in .wash/plan.toml). Two packages landed today on the same gap. B61: Io::connect at MAX_WAITERS waits HAND_US (1 s) for an ended piped's waiter to hand its place over; under heavy load a hand-over took longer and the seventh fresh piped was refused Emfile though no live server held its place (pipe-sixty-four rv32 beside a whole bench, 1 run in 3), so that case stays in the quiet class. B51, your ruling on B51-completion-hold: the client's completion call has no clock; a Hub::wait caller overshoots its deadline by a stall; a wedged-alive server holds its clients until it dies. Both want a wait bounded by the request's own deadline, and beamlet's files layer (userland/otp/redoubt/src/files.rs, Io) carries none: an Elixir File call has no deadline to tie to, and the serving library's hold is the server's. Questions: (1) where a request's deadline lives: the Elixir caller's timeout (File.* take none today; a per-call option? the process's receive timeout?), the session's (one bound for the VM's I/O), or a per-connection default the hub applies; (2) what a hub wait does at it: answer late-busy to the caller (the file call fails with a named reason, the connection kept), refuse, or hand the place back; (3) whether the kernel's R28 parked-call accounting or the server-side hold is touched (your B51 ruling says no server change); (4) what pipe-sixty-four and the two hub tests become once the bound exists (B61's 1 s becomes the deadline's remainder?). Reply on this thread with the rule, the page sentences, and the cases; b51-implementer builds it (Tier A, steward-red-2).

### Architect · Answer

Event: `833e1776cade7b7717aa2a4612508382` · 2026-10-15T07:44:18Z

> B62 ruling: one clock, the files layer's, off the VM's thread. Full text, page sentences, cases: .wash/local/B62-ruling.md.
> 
> Correction: B51's Hub::wait residual does not reach beamlet; it never calls Hub::wait (a waiter thread per connection, io.rs:89; the VM idles in Io::wait). What B51 leaves beamlet: a request in a stalled server has no client bound, and a `busy` request retries every RETRY_US for ever (files.rs:291). B61's wait is on the VM's thread. Both gaps are one: the files layer has no clock on what an operation waits for.
> 
> Rule: `FILE_WAIT_US` = 10 s (COLLECT_WAIT's value, the server's own longest wait for a request: both clocks agree). Per request from its first send, busy retries counted; from the op's start for a place at MAX_WAITERS. At the bound the op fails to its asker with the name of what it waited for: an answer → Name::Timeout → `etimedout` (files.rs:897), a later answer dropped, the fid retired as Reply::Lost's, connection kept; a share → `busy`, retries stop; a place → `emfile`. Io::connect at the bound no longer blocks: the op parks wanting a place (as a busy one parks for its retry), connects when a hand-over frees one (the VM loop already takes them) or fails at its deadline. HAND_US stays for pool/jobs; B61's hand_over on the connect path goes.
> 
> (1) The platform's constant, like MAX_CALL_MS; not File.*'s (OTP API), not the process's, not the session's (one slow volume must not fail the console). (2) Fail named, connection kept; never refuse early, never hand back (B47 stands). (3) R28, the server hold, requests_wait, the clockless completion call: untouched. (4) The two hub tests rewritten (connect returns Later at once; emfile at FILE_WAIT_US on the fake clock), two new (unanswered → etimedout, late answer dropped, next op serves; busy for ever → busy, retries stop). pipe-sixty-four leaves the quiet class only after 5 rv32 runs beside a whole bench pass: measure, the hand-over is still load-bound.
