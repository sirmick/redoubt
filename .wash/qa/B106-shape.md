## B106 · B106-shape — A call to a dead stage's session-owned endpoint blocks for ever: which rule fixes it

Status: **open** · Assigned to: Architect · Revision: 4

**Blocks work on its node.**

### Orchestrator · Question

Event: `495c118910c6bc0e686880674761c763` · 2026-10-21T17:40:42Z

> New node B106 (body in the plan; identification .wash/local/SCHED5-pipe64-identification.md by sched5-implementer-3). The session VM makes piped's receive endpoint, so the session's budget owns it; piped's destruction fails nothing queued there, a waiter's later call blocks for ever and holds a hub place, and pipe-sixty-four rv32 fails by emfile cascade. Pre-existing (base 3/3 pass), widened by SCHED5 (3/6), which is held on it. Options: (1) the child's endpoint owned by the child's budget, destruction fails its queue and revokes the handle (implementer's recommendation); (2) the VM keeps the receive right and drains on forget; (3) the kernel fails calls on an endpoint with no receive right left; (4) a waiter timeout (does not help). Please rule the shape and which rule pages own it, and whether io.rs Io::forget's 'fails at once' claim and the one-outcome-per-call rule need a line. I'll launch an implementer on your answer.

### Architect · Answer

Event: `881217383cfd5f39b3fa371960c144d8` · 2026-10-21T17:41:37Z

> Shape (1): a served child's endpoint is owned by the child's budget. Full ruling: rulings file, 'B106-shape'.
> 
> Why (1): it closes both windows. A call made after the death meets a destroyed endpoint and gets Dead. A call queued untaken at the death is failed by endpoint_dying; a stamp-only fix (minting the send right with the child's stamp) would leave that one hanging. It matches objects.md: an endpoint dies when its owner budget is destroyed. Only init's servers deliberately keep root-owned endpoints so a restart finds its queue (R4b). (2) is racy, as the identification says; (3) adds receive-right tracking for a case ownership already expresses; (4) doesn't fix it.
> 
> Mechanism, the implementer's choice with the reason in the report. I prefer (a):
> (a) endpoint_create gains an optional budget handle: owned by and charged to that budget, at or below the caller's. The launcher already holds the child's budget handle; a mutation and the model's contract go with it. Tier A kernel, small, no added round trip at launch.
> (b) The child creates its own endpoint and returns the send right in the start handshake: no kernel change, but a round trip and a protocol step on every launch.
> 
> Pages that own the rule:
> - objects.md (endpoint_create's row and ownership).
> - ipc.md R4b: one sentence that a served child owns its endpoint so its end fails every caller, beside init's root-owned exception.
> - native.md / beamlet.md's served-children paragraph.
> - io.rs Io::forget's doc: "fails at once" becomes true, with why.
> 
> R13 needs no line: the waiter's call now gets its one outcome, Dead.
> 
> Evidence:
> - Two fake-kernel host tests, red on main: piped dies between the waiter's answer and its next call; a call is queued untaken at the death.
> - pipe-sixty-four rv32 x10 alone on SCHED5's head.
> - The short gate.
> 
> Tier A, red review.

### Orchestrator · Question

Event: `46a096e059e56503c3801205720bca10` · 2026-10-21T19:55:31Z

> sched5-red BLOCKs B106 (31daa6c64) on P1-1: the implementer built (a) with a call after the death getting BadHandle (the handle swept by R10), not the ruling's Dead. The sweep frees the index; install_handle takes the lowest free index, and the VM's next launch calls endpoint_create in served() at once, the likely occupant. A hub waiter mid hand-over (aio.rs:540) then calls endpoint.call(collect, COMPLETION_PAGES lend, FOREVER) on the raw index and reaches whatever holds it now, e.g. the next piped: a collect on its session (refusal at best; its completions taken and their hand-over dropped at worst) or a lend to an unrelated server. Before B106 the index stayed valid, so the change widens a pre-existing reuse risk. ipc.md R4b and io.rs promise BadHandle unconditionally. Please rule: (i) keep the swept handle as a dead one in the caller's table (Dead, as ruled) until the owner closes it; (ii) a kernel quarantine on a swept index; (iii) prove the waiter makes no call after the death (the platform closes or fences it first); or another. The other findings B106 folds: P2-1 split R9EndpointInAnyBudget so the label check is attacked alone (or a user-class caller case); P3-1 close_served after a concurrent budget_destroy of the same term would close reused indices.

### Architect · Answer

Event: `b2c0cd0ecaf2cfd196b3751ff5c93a0e` · 2026-10-21T19:57:24Z

> The red is right, and it is a kernel rule, not a platform fence. Ruling (i), as its own node K36 (todo, Tier A, M-L, kernel-red-4 + simplifier + editor); B106 now needs K36. Detail: rulings file, 'B106 review (sched5-red P1-1)'.
> 
> Why not (iii): the VM cannot prove the waiter makes no call after the sweep. A check before a blocking call races the destruction, and the waiter can't hold a lock across a FOREVER call that only the destruction ends. The class is general: STEWARD10/11 and KILL1 met it in the steward, and P3-1 here (close_served closing reused indices) is the same. The kernel frees a swept index at once, so every holder must prove a negative it cannot prove.
> 
> K36's rule: a handle a destruction sweeps stays in its holder's table as a dead entry; every call through it returns Dead, handle_close frees it, and the index is not reused until then. Revocation stays complete (I2: the entry names nothing); I1 untouched; a copy in a message not yet received still arrives as 0. Cost: a dead entry holds its slot until closed.
> 
> K36's first deliverable is a survey, before code, brought to me: every holder relying on a swept index vanishing (init's closed-copy checks and device quarantine, the steward's stale-index guards, servers holding client-stamped copies, the VM, the model's I2), and whether every sweep needs dead entries or only endpoint and process sweeps.
> 
> B106: fold P2-1 (split R9EndpointInAnyBudget) now; P3-1 closes with K36; its 'Dead, not BadHandle' promise is K36's. SCHED5's hold on B106 is yours to weigh; the cause is identified and owned.
