## B98 · B98-cause — B98: blkd's 10 s REQUEST_TIMEOUT_US under a loaded host's clock

Status: **open** · Assigned to: Architect · Revision: 2

### Implementer · Question

Event: `f979ae6efcbb4a8e320a061a4552ac01` · 2026-10-20T23:18:26Z

> B98's cause is reproduced. The numbers are in ./.tmp/B98/cause-numbers.md; logs are in .tmp/B98/inject/.
> 
> With host I/O pressure injected (a pinned dd 2 GB + fsync loop on the root ext4 that holds the case images), beamlet-files failed 3/10 on each width. Twice blkd said `the device is marked broken: a flush: no completion in 10001367 us (the bound is 10000000), and nothing since` (10000376-10002228 us across 4 consoles). At mount, walfsd said `why it does not mount: formatting the blank range: Io: blkd answered Failed`. Both timeouts fall inside the host writer's 11.7 s and 13.9 s fsync passes, with PSI io avg10 at 53-78 % and Writeback up to 2 GB.
> 
> No byte was wrong; it is not a kernel or driver finding. The guest's flush is QEMU's fdatasync (cache=writeback), queued behind the host's writeback. There is no icount, so blkd's 10 s was 10 real host seconds. Under natural load with PSI at 2.8 % it did not reproduce in 20 runs.
> 
> A second 10 s bound sits on the same path: the files layer's FILE_WAIT_US (= COLLECT_WAIT) gave beamlet etimedout once with no blkd break, because a walfs transaction is several writes and four flushes.
> 
> The questions:
> (1) Is a missed REQUEST_TIMEOUT_US a lie? With one request outstanding, blkd could fail the request but keep it tracked and wait for its completion before the next submit. A late completion would then be expected, and only protocol violations would be permanent.
> (2) Or does the bench own it? Case disks could use cache=unsafe, so a guest flush costs no host fsync. They are fresh per boot, and launch-keep-disk's second boot uses the same file.
> (3) Does FILE_WAIT_US get the same answer?
> 
> No bound has moved. I'm waiting for your ruling before building anything.

### Architect · Answer

Event: `6a81936c88bee7e03a0e5863d37bbf2c` · 2026-10-20T23:19:52Z

> A clean identification; thank you for not building first. Ruling (detail: rulings file, 'B98-cause'):
> 
> (1) No: the box's rule stands. "A lie is permanent" has a stated reason that is sound for DMA (a completion for a request no longer tracked), and a 10 s flush on the box's own SSD is a broken device. Your refinement is coherent, but it answers a condition the box's hardware does not present, and it would not rescue the volume anyway: walfsd poisons on a Failed write, correctly. A node only if real hardware shows a slow-but-honest device. No bound moves.
> 
> (2) Yes: the bench owns it. A case's disk is fresh per boot, and the guest's flush ordering is honoured in the host's page cache either way: a QEMU exit loses nothing, and the power-loss case simulates loss by QEMU's exit, not the host's. So the bench's case disks run cache=unsafe: a guest flush costs no host fsync and the host's writeback pressure no longer reaches blkd's bound. launch-keep-disk's second boot reads the same page cache. ./launch, the owner's hand runs, keeps writeback: its disks and its host are the user's; state that, it is not decided here. testbench.md's disk section states the setting and the reason with your numbers (3/10 at PSI 53-78 %, 0/20 at 2.8 %); blkd.md says that on a virtualised disk the bound measures the host too, which is why the bench does not fsync it.
> 
> (3) The same answer. FILE_WAIT_US does not move for a host artefact; a walfs transaction exceeding it on the box's own disk would be a finding of its own.
> 
> The reason lines stay (blkd's broken line with cause and numbers, walfsd's mount reason): they made this identification possible and are B98's deliverable beside the bench change; console lines are contracts, so name the cases that expect or forbid them. Gate: beamlet-files and walfsd-power-loss both widths under your injected pressure (0 of 10), the disk cases unchanged, the short gate. Tier A as assigned, reviewer unchanged.
