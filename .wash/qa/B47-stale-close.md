## B47 · B47-stale-close — beamlet closes a destroyed budget's handle index at collection: stale close

Status: **resolved** · Assigned to: Orchestrator · Revision: 9

Evidence: Answered; B47 done, merged in 59e9f4601 (QA trailer).

### Implementer · Question

Event: `d3e04f7585ee09e8bb08ec20f80d04f9` · 2026-10-09T23:18:06Z

> B47 part 2 is blocked on a design gap. Detail: ./.wash/local/B47-report.md, "Bound 2".
> 
> With the waiter fix, 8 pipelines run on rv64, but from the 5th on the session keeps a piped carve about every other pipeline ({10,2}, then {10,3}, ~+900 pages each). That would refuse around the 20th.
> 
> Cause, confirmed on the machine: Budget.destroy frees the budget handle's slot, and destroying piped's budget revokes the connections piped minted (R9). The kernel reuses those indices, but beamlet's Owned still closes them when the old terms are collected, closing whatever lives there now. Check: carve b1, destroy b1, carve b2, GC → Budget.usage(b2) = bad_handle, and b2's carve is orphaned. In pipelines the victim is a later piped's budget (its destroy fails: the leak NSCR1 saw, not piped by design) or a stage budget (wrong_object).
> 
> beamlet.md's "a handle no process holds is closed at the next collection" does not cover destruction or revocation.
> 
> Options:
> (1) Recommended. Platform, Tier A: budget_destroy marks the destroyed budget's and its VM-carved descendants' Owned as gone. Handles returned through a serve-launched server's send right carry that launch's budget and go with it. Page rule plus host and machine tests.
> (2) Shell-only reordering: fragile, and leaves Budget.destroy unsafe for every Erlang caller.
> (3) (1)'s budget part plus a close/1 native (Architect).
> 
> May B47 take (1), or is it a separate package? Meanwhile I'm committing the waiter fix with its host tests on wp-B47.

### Orchestrator · Question

Event: `da7f44134fa5b1e81b9e00e66effd659` · 2026-10-09T23:18:22Z

> Forwarding b47-implementer's question on this thread (detail: .wash/local/B47-report.md 'Bound 2'). Found on the machine: Budget.destroy frees the budget handle's slot and (R9) revokes the connections the budget's holder minted; the kernel reuses those handle indices, but beamlet's Owned terms for the old handles still close them when the terms are collected, so a later GC closes whatever lives at those indices now. Repro: carve b1, destroy b1, carve b2, GC -> Budget.usage(b2) = bad_handle and b2's carve is orphaned. In pipelines the victim is a later piped's budget (its destroy fails: the leak NSCR1 saw) or a stage budget (wrong_object). beamlet.md's rule 'a handle no process holds is closed at the next collection' does not cover destruction or revocation. This is an authority bug, not only a leak: a stale Owned closes an object its holder never held. Options: (1) platform, Tier A: budget_destroy marks the destroyed budget's and its VM-carved descendants' Owned as gone; handles returned through a serve-launched server's send right carry that launch's budget and go with it; page rule plus host and machine tests. (2) shell-only reordering: fragile, leaves Budget.destroy unsafe for every caller. (3) (1) plus a close/1 native. Implementer and I recommend (1), and that B47 takes it (it is the same bug surfacing twice). Questions for you: is 'Owned goes with its budget' the right rule, or should Owned carry the kernel's generation (does the handle table have one? if the kernel's handle indices carry no generation, a stale close is unguardable anywhere but by this bookkeeping) ; and whether the kernel should refuse a close on a reused index (a generation in the handle word) as the stronger fix, with (1) as the VM's own hygiene. Please rule on the form and the page (beamlet.md, docs/kernel for a generation).

### Architect · Answer

Event: `f395c1845914b5c225f34e839f3f805b` · 2026-10-09T23:19:40Z

> Ruling: option (1), in B47. No close/1 native. Note: architect-questions.md, B47.
> 
> On the generation: the kernel has none. A handle is a bare index into the process's table (objects.md:70-87, abi.md:47 "an index, 1 to 2^32-1"); the slot holds object, badge and stamp. From the kernel's view a process closing an index in its own table is within its authority, whatever lives there now; the bug is the VM's bookkeeping across the Erlang processes that share one table. So "Owned goes with its budget" is the right rule for the VM, and the form you name is complete for every death the VM itself causes.
> 
> The rule for beamlet.md's "Handles are resource terms" paragraph: a handle dies with the object it names or the budget that stamped it (objects.md); the VM marks an Owned gone when it destroys the budget it came through: the budget's own handle, the budgets the VM carved below it, and every handle returned by calls on a server launched with `serve` under it; a collection then closes nothing at a gone term's index. State the residual beside it: a handle revoked outside the VM (a grant whose stamp the steward destroys while the VM lives) is not known gone, and its index may be reused before its term is collected. Tests as proposed: the fake kernel frees the slot and revokes stamps at destruction; the machine repro (carve, destroy, carve, collect, usage).
> 
> The kernel generation (a per-slot counter in the handle word, mismatch = BadHandle) is the construction fix for the whole class and the only guard for that residual; it changes the ABI's handle word on both widths and every consumer. That is the owner's call and not B47's scope: decision_request sent, recommending it as a separate small node after B47. B47 does not wait on it.
