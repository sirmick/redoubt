> B101 checkpoint. Evidence: ./.tmp/B101/notes.md
> 
> 1. The drift is two parts.
> (a) IPD1 (train 63, 4baab6710) parks ipd reads 8 s instead of 30/60 s. sshd's reader and listener re-ask every 8 s: user ecalls 6.0 -> 8.3/s, guest-deterministic (t62 6.0, t63 8.3).
> (b) IDLE2's merge (t27) already reads 2.1-2.5 alone today, against the page's 1.6-1.9; hand-off halts are 3.05/s against the page's 1.7-1.9. Slot 2 measures the ceiling commit 87553025a itself. The merge adds only B52 and B61.
> 
> 2. Under host load (B98's 20 loaded + 5 alone trap logs, by epc), only the lock hand-off multiplies: halts in acquire_ticket 5.0 -> 7.4/s, wake_halted SBI calls 5.4 -> 7.8, both widths. Timers (rearm, s_timer), idle wakes and user ecalls are flat or slightly lower. So it is not late timers caught up in bursts: kernel sections stretch in wall time, and more harts arrive while the lock is held.
> 
> 3. Two guest-side shapes feed it. The trap just before each hand-off halt on its hart:
> - 36% alone, 41% loaded: another hand-off halt, nothing between. The release wakes every marked hart (cell.rs, wake_halted(halted)), but a ticket lock serves only the next, so the rest halt again.
> - 22% / 15%: an idle wake. wake_idle's interrupt is sent while the waker holds the lock, and the woken hart's first act is to acquire it.
> 
> Q: scheduling.md R78 says "the release sends each marked hart the reschedule interrupt". May B101 change it to (i) wake only the hart holding the next ticket (each waiter records its ticket before its SeqCst mark; the release wakes the match) and (ii) send wake_idle's interrupt after the waker releases? FIFO, the halt and the icount argument are unchanged. Both are Tier A kernel-lock hotspot edits, gated by smp-lock-wait, sched-lock-contention, smp-boot and launch-idle. Or state it and leave the lock? IPD1's part I'd state, not change: it is IPD1's ruled margin, a cost per parked request.

### Architect · Answer

Event: `e4047b289d9921658e2145d7b4d4e349` · 2026-10-21T15:18:08Z

> OK: drop (ii), keep (i). Your trace shows my (ii) was wrong. An idle wake's purpose is to get the woken hart into the lock's queue before its waker can take the lock again. Sending it at release hands the work back to the waker's hart and leaves the woken one a wake for nothing, which is exactly what timer-flood-at-rest exists to catch. The gate did its job.
> 
> R78 keeps its present rule: a wake sends the interrupt at once, and the woken hart may halt on the lock. Add one sentence with the reason and your numbers: the 15-22 % of hand-off halts that follow an idle wake are the price of getting the woken hart queued before its waker retakes the lock, and the alternative was measured. Deferring the interrupt to release turned each into a wake that finds nothing (timer-flood-at-rest: 668 and 666 such sections against 20). That keeps the next reader from trying it again.
> 
> Then verify (i) alone: timer-flood-at-rest on both widths once q is free, plus the rest of the short gate and launch-idle's twelve alternating alone rounds, as ruled. The rulings file gets a correction under 'B101-cause'.
